Learn the towns

The songs open the door. The curriculum walks through it - the full, sourced history of every town, built for learners, families, and classrooms.

Every All Black Towns song starts as research. The Town Dossiers are where that research lives in full: each one tells a town's complete story - its founding and founders, its churches, schools, and businesses, the turning point that changed everything, and what stands there today - with a timeline, a key facts box, primary sources, and the song's lyrics annotated line by line.

Lesson plans turn each dossier into teaching: a lesson opens with the song, moves into the documents, and closes with discussion and a hands-on task. The pilot lesson, built on Sweet Auburn in Atlanta, is complete; the full curriculum grows town by town from the dossiers, toward every town on the master list.

Washington

Louisiana

A St. Landry Parish steamboat town on Bayou Courtableau, begun as a French trading post in 1764, where Black landowners kept farms and river landings after the Civil War.

Parting Ways

Massachusetts

Rural African American settlement in Plymouth, founded in 1792 when the town granted about 94 acres to veterans of the American Revolution.

Mossville

Louisiana

One of the oldest free Black communities in the Louisiana Purchase territory, founded in 1793 by Jim Moss and twelve Black families on the Calcasieu Prairie west of present-day Lake Charles.

Read the full dossier →

Tremé

Louisiana

A New Orleans neighborhood just north of the French Quarter, widely considered the oldest African American neighborhood in the United States: founded in 1810 as Faubourg Tremé, anchored by Congo Square and St. Augustine Church, and a cradle of early jazz.

Read the full dossier →

Five Points

New York

Five Points was one of New York City's first free Black settlements, raised in lower Manhattan over the drained Collect Pond. Free Black families built one of the young city's earliest Black communities there in the early 1800s.

Read the full dossier →

Sandy Spring

Maryland

Sandy Spring began as a Quaker village; after Friends freed most enslaved people by about 1820, Black families built a church in 1822. Their settlement became the county's largest free Black community and an Underground Railroad haven.
