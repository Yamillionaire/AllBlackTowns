Learn the towns

The songs open the door. The curriculum walks through it - the full, sourced history of every town, built for learners, families, and classrooms.

Every All Black Towns song starts as research. The Town Dossiers are where that research lives in full: each one tells a town's complete story - its founding and founders, its churches, schools, and businesses, the turning point that changed everything, and what stands there today - with a timeline, a key facts box, primary sources, and the song's lyrics annotated line by line.

Lesson plans turn each dossier into teaching: a lesson opens with the song, moves into the documents, and closes with discussion and a hands-on task. The pilot lesson, built on Sweet Auburn in Atlanta, is complete; the full curriculum grows town by town from the dossiers, toward every town on the master list.

The pilot collection

Five towns, five complete dossiers - the first set, finished and verified against the sources.

Sweet Auburn

Atlanta, Georgia

The historic Black business district once called the richest Negro street in the world - and the home of the pilot lesson plan.

Boley

Oklahoma

One of the largest and most famous all-Black towns of the West, founded in 1903 and still an incorporated town today.

Nicodemus

Kansas

Founded in 1877 by Black settlers from Kentucky; the oldest surviving all-Black town west of the Mississippi, now a National Historic Site.

Eatonville

Florida

Incorporated in 1887, among the first self-governing all-Black towns in America - and the hometown of Zora Neale Hurston.

Rosewood

Florida

A thriving town destroyed by racial violence in 1923. Its dossier keeps the full record of what was lost.
