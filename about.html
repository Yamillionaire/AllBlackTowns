About the project

After Emancipation, Black Americans built hundreds of their own towns - municipalities, districts, and settlements across the country, founded on the belief that freedom should come with land, self-rule, and a future.

Many thrived. Boley, Oklahoma. Nicodemus, Kansas. Blackdom, New Mexico. Mound Bayou, Mississippi. Then came the violence, the policy, the highways, the "urban renewal" - and town after town was destroyed, dispersed, or paved over.

"They tried to bury the towns. We're singing their names back."

All Black Towns is a creative universe built on true stories, honoring every one of them - one town, one song, one story at a time. Each song carries the town's real history in its lyrics, so the names live in melody even where the buildings are gone.

The project is also a living archive: the lyrics are documented, the research is sourced, and the catalog grows as more towns are found and more songs are written.

This is history you can hear.

About the creator

Yamil Little is an Atlanta-based creative director and the co-founder of The Elements of Hip Hop, the company behind HipHopolis, a superhero universe powered by the elements of hip hop. He created All Black Towns to put the names of America's historic Black towns back into the culture, as songs people can carry with them.

His career moves between music, media, and community work.

The project is personal. Little's grandparents came from Alabama, and the towns in these songs are the kind of places his own family's story runs through. All Black Towns is his way of keeping the record alive: researched from the sources, written town by town, and sung back into memory.
