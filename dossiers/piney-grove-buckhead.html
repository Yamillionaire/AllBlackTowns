Key Facts Box

  • Founded: c. 1825, carried as a CLOSEST ESTIMATE. The community and its cemetery likely date to the mid-1820s, with Piney Grove Baptist Church dated to 1827; inside the same range, a summary of Atlanta Journal-Constitution reporting gives the cemetery breaking ground in 1826 as the graveyard of Piney Grove Baptist Church. The estimate is an estimate, and the 1826 and 1827 specifics are carried openly.
  • Founded by: NOT ESTABLISHED. No source consulted names a founder, a founding family, a land seller, or a land purchase for Piney Grove, its church, or its cemetery. The earliest people the record names are the enslaved and later emancipated people buried in the cemetery, as a class rather than as named founders.
  • Incorporated: never. Piney Grove was a community or settlement in Buckhead, not a municipality, and it never had a town government. Buckhead itself was unincorporated for most of Piney Grove's life and was annexed by the City of Atlanta in 1952.
  • Peak population: NOT ESTABLISHED. No source publishes any population count for Piney Grove at any date. The only numbered human measure in the record is the cemetery itself: over 300 graves. A grave count is not a population count, and this dossier does not convert one into the other.
  • Population today: no resident community. Piney Grove is a vanished community; what remains on the ground is the cemetery at 834 Canterbury Road, Atlanta, its descendant organization, and the surrounding Buckhead development.
  • Known for: being one of the earliest African American communities in Buckhead, called in the Burnaway account the site of "Buckhead's earliest African American community," surviving now only in its cemetery: a burial ground of over 300 graves, including burials believed to be of enslaved people and headstones for individuals born before the Emancipation Proclamation, determined eligible for the National Register of Historic Places, listed by the Georgia Trust for Historic Preservation among its 2024 Places in Peril, and restored to a court-protected footing by its descendants in 2025.
  • The song: "Piney Grove-Buckhead, Georgia," this town's song in the All Black Towns series.

Introduction

  • Town name, State: Piney Grove-Buckhead, Georgia
  • County: Fulton County, Georgia
  • Status: vanished former African American community or settlement in Buckhead, Atlanta. Its cemetery survives at 834 Canterbury Road as its last remaining historic landmark; the church building was demolished after storm damage in 1996, and no resident community remains.
  • Dossier version: draft v0.1, 2026-11-09

Piney Grove was not an incorporated town. It was a former African American community or settlement in Buckhead, in what is now the northern part of the City of Atlanta, known today through Piney Grove Baptist Church, also carried as Piney Grove Missionary Baptist Church, and through its cemetery. The cemetery is the community's last remaining historic landmark: the church building is gone, and no house or street of the settlement survives in the record.

Its founding is a date range with a church and a graveyard inside it, not a founding act with a founder. The Black Cemetery Network and the Buckhead Heritage Society both date the community to the mid-1820s, and the project carries c. 1825 as a closest estimate. Inside that range, a summary of Atlanta Journal-Constitution reporting gives the cemetery breaking ground in 1826 as the graveyard of Piney Grove Baptist Church, and a heritage-preservation account dates the congregation's establishment to 1827. No source names a founder, a founding family, or a land purchase. The founding generation is documented through the graves: over 300 burials, including individuals born before the Emancipation Proclamation and burials believed to be of enslaved people.

The church stood at the community's center for well over a century, burying Black Atlantans whether or not they were members. Its first building was condemned in 1948, its second was demolished after storm damage in 1996, and the homes of the settlement pass out of the written record undescribed. What remained, the cemetery, was then threatened by the development rising around it, until the descendants of the people buried there organized, won recognition, and in 2025 won a court judgment that funds its restoration and requires its maintenance in perpetuity. The chapters that follow take the story in order, from the founding to the town today.

Timeline: the story at a glance

Mid-1820sthe Piney Grove community and its cemetery likely date to this period (Black Cemetery Network; Buckhead Heritage Society). The project carries c. 1825 as a closest estimate.
1826the cemetery broke ground in this year as the graveyard of Piney Grove Baptist Church, by the summary of Atlanta Journal-Constitution reporting carried in The Trendr.
1827the Piney Grove congregation was established in this year (Burnaway, 2013). The church is also carried under the fuller name Piney Grove Missionary Baptist Church.
1820s through the 1990sburials continued at Piney Grove Cemetery across this whole span (Burnaway, 2013): over 300 graves, including individuals born before the Emancipation Proclamation and burials believed to be of enslaved people.
1948the original Piney Grove church building was condemned; members worshipped outside until a new church was completed.
1952Atlanta annexed Buckhead. The mechanism and dates of the residential community's disappearance are not established in the sources.
1996the second church building's roof collapsed during a storm, and the building was demolished after the damage. After 1996 no church building stood; the cemetery remained.
Early 2000sa residential developer acquired the former church property and sought to remove the cemetery; after opposition by the descendants, the land was sold with conditions for cemetery access and maintenance, and a condominium complex was built adjacent.
2006the Buckhead Coalition fought to ensure permanent public access to the cemetery, and the City of Atlanta's November 2006 ordinances required clear access, homeowners association maintenance, and a perpetual access easement.
March 2023the nonprofit Friends of Piney Grove Cemetery, Inc. was launched by descendants Audrey Collins and Rhonda Jackson to save the cemetery and preserve its history.
November 2023the Georgia Trust for Historic Preservation released its 2024 list of 10 Places in Peril, including Piney Grove Cemetery.
October to November 2025the Fulton County Superior Court granted the descendants summary judgment, the parties settled, and the November 3, 2025 Final Consent Judgment set the terms: restoration funded by the homeowners association, and maintenance in perpetuity.

The Song

Piney Grove was a 19th-century Black community in Buckhead, Atlanta. Its cemetery, dating to the 1820s with more than 300 graves, is its last visible remnant. It was one of several Black enclaves that once dotted Buckhead.
