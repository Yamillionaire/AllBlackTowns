Key Facts Box

  • Founded: 1822, carried as DOCUMENTED. In 1822 the Sharp Street congregation was founded, the first African American congregation and the oldest African American church in Montgomery County, and land was conveyed for the Black community's house of worship and burial ground. The free Black settlement itself is older, and the Quaker village underneath it older still.
  • Founded by: no single founder in the town sense. The Black community was founded in effect by the free Black families themselves: the formerly enslaved people manumitted by local Friends and the free families who joined them, who built the church, bought land, and planted the settlement's institutions. The Quaker village underneath was settled by Friends including James Brooke and John Thomas, who received land from their father-in-law Richard Snowden.
  • Incorporated: never. Sandy Spring is an unincorporated community in Montgomery County, Maryland, and has never had a municipal government of its own.
  • Peak population: not established. No source publishes a population count for the historic Black settlement of Sandy Spring alone; the earliest scholarly study of the community states that the census returns for Sandy Spring could not be separated from the surrounding district.
  • Population today: 5,746 in the 2020 U.S. Census, for the Ashton-Sandy Spring census-designated place, which combines Ashton, Sandy Spring, and Brinklow. No modern figure describes the historic Black settlement alone.
  • Known for: one of the earliest and largest free Black communities in Maryland, formed after Sandy Spring Friends manumitted the people they enslaved decades before state or federal emancipation; Sharp Street Church (1822), the oldest African American church in Montgomery County; the county's first school for Black children, held at Sharp Street about 1864; an Underground Railroad haven; generations of Black builders, farmers, and educators; and two preserved Black cemeteries, Mutual Memorial (1873) and Ash Memorial (1897).
  • The song: "Sandy Spring, Maryland," this town's song in the All Black Towns series.

Introduction

  • Town name, State: Sandy Spring, Maryland
  • County: Montgomery County, Maryland
  • Status: unincorporated community. The historic Black settlement survives as a living community inside the larger unincorporated Sandy Spring area: its church, its two historic cemeteries, its lodge hall, and descendant families remain in place.
  • Dossier version: draft v0.1, 2026-11-09

Sandy Spring has three foundings stacked on one piece of ground, and its whole meaning is in their order: a Quaker village, the freeing of the people its Quakers enslaved, and the Black community's own founding. The land was patented first: Richard Snowden patented Snowden's Manor, 1,000 acres, in 1715, and his sons-in-law James Brooke and John Thomas settled the Quaker village about 1728 near the headwaters of the Anacostia and Patuxent rivers. The Friends Meeting is recorded from 1753.

The freeing came by religious discipline applied over decades. The Society of Friends prohibited its members from owning enslaved people in the 1770s, and after 1781 local Friends who failed to free the people they held were disowned. Richard Thomas's 1806 provision freed 112 people, and by about 1820 most Sandy Spring Quakers had freed the people they enslaved. That support stopped short of equality: Black residents were never invited into full membership in the Meeting, so the community that followed was built separately, by free Black families themselves.

In 1822 the Sharp Street congregation was founded, the first African American congregation in Montgomery County, and land was conveyed for the community's house of worship and burial ground, joining worship and burial from the first day. The chapters that follow take the story in order, from the founding to the town today.

Timeline: the story at a glance

1715Richard Snowden patents 1,000 acres as Snowden's Manor in the Sandy Spring area; the tract is later enlarged and surveyed at over 9,000 acres.
c. 1728James Brooke and John Thomas, sons-in-law of Richard Snowden, build houses in what becomes Sandy Spring; the Society of Architectural Historians dates the founding of the Quaker community to about 1728 and calls Sandy Spring the county's oldest community.
1753the Sandy Spring Friends Meeting has a recorded existence from 1753, and its burying ground dates to the same decade.
1770s to 1781the Society of Friends prohibits its members from owning enslaved people; after 1781, Sandy Spring Friends who failed to free the people they held were disowned.
1806Quaker Richard Thomas provides for the manumission of 112 enslaved people, who become the nucleus of the Black community called Cincinnati, near Brinklow.
By about 1820most Sandy Spring Quakers have freed the people they enslaved, creating a significant free Black population in the area.
1822the Sharp Street congregation is founded, the first African American congregation in Montgomery County, and land is conveyed for the Black community's church and burial ground. This is the project's documented founding year for Sandy Spring.
About 1864a school for Black children, the first in Montgomery County, is held in Sharp Street Church.
1873Mutual Memorial Cemetery is officially established as part of the historic Freedman's Village of Sandy Spring.
1897the Sisters Mutual Aid Society purchases land for Ash Memorial Cemetery.
1906 to 1909the Sandy Spring Odd Fellows Lodge, Lodge No. 6430 of the Grand United Order of Odd Fellows, is built.
1923the present Sharp Street church building is erected in brick, after the frame church built in 1886 burned in 1920.

The Song

Sandy Spring began as a Quaker village; after Friends freed most enslaved people by about 1820, Black families built a church in 1822. Their settlement became the county's largest free Black community and an Underground Railroad haven.
