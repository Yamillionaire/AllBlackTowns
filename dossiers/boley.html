Boley, Oklahoma - Town Dossier (Sample Extract)

Beta sample extract. The opening of the Boley, Oklahoma dossier: its header, key facts, and founding story. The full dossier is part of the Pilot Collection.

Header

  • Town name, State: Boley, Oklahoma
  • County: Okfuskee County
  • Status: still incorporated. One of thirteen surviving all-Black towns in Oklahoma, of more than fifty founded in the state (Oklahoma Historical Society). The downtown business district is the Boley Historic District, listed in the National Register of Historic Places and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1975 (National Park Service).
  • Dossier version: pilot draft v0.1, 2026-10-26

Key Facts Box

  • Founded: 1903. The townsite was established in August 1903 and opened for settlement on September 26, 1903 (Oklahoma Historical Society marker; town history summarized from the National Register nomination). A formal opening celebration followed on September 22, 1904 (National Register nomination). See conflict 1.
  • Founded by: Thomas M. Haynes, a Black farmer and entrepreneur from Texas, chosen as townsite manager and the town's principal founder, working with Lake Moore, a white speculator who put up the initial investment, and J. B. Boley, a white official of the Fort Smith and Western Railway, for whom the town was named. The townsite stood on the land allotment of Abigail Barnett, a Creek freedwoman (National Register nomination; Oklahoma Historical Society; BlackPast).
  • Incorporated: May 11, 1905 (Oklahoma Historical Society marker).
  • Peak population: NOT ESTABLISHED as a single figure. Contemporary and later accounts claim more than 4,000 people by 1911 (Oklahoma Historical Society), and the state historical marker gives 4,000 citizens with 25,000 more in the surrounding area; the town's own commemorative plaque says 4,200. Those figures appear to count Boley together with its farm trade territory. The incorporated town's highest decennial census count was 1,334 (1910). Do not publish a peak number without its basis attached. See conflict 4.
  • Population today: 1,091 (2020 census). The Oklahoma Historical Society encyclopedia gives 1,087 for the same census; see conflict 5. The census figure includes the roughly 800 inmates of the John H. Lilley Correctional Center in Boley (Oklahoma Department of Corrections), so it overstates the size of the town's civilian community.
  • Known for: the largest and best known of Oklahoma's all-Black towns; Booker T. Washington's 1905 praise; the town that defeated Pretty Boy Floyd's gang at its own bank in 1932; the nation's oldest African American community-based rodeo (Oklahoma Historical Society).
  • The song: "Boley, Oklahoma" (the original's own Suno title; "IRON TRACKS TO BOLEY" is the title of the May 2026 remaster; see Section 7 and the correction in the Research log).

1. Founding

Boley began as a railroad townsite with an argument behind it. In the spring of 1903, in a discussion about whether Black people were capable of self-government, J. B. Boley, a roadmaster with the Fort Smith and Western Railway, argued that they had simply never had a fair chance; the town that resulted was, in Booker T. Washington's telling, laid out to put that claim to the test (Washington, "Boley, a Negro Town in the West," Outlook, January 4, 1908; the story is also carried in the National Register nomination).

The site was chosen on the Fort Smith and Western line, roughly halfway between Paden and Castle in what was then the Creek Nation, Indian Territory (Oklahoma Historical Society). The land was the 160-acre allotment of Abigail Barnett, who had inherited it from her father James Barnett, a Creek freedman (National Register nomination). Lake Moore, a white attorney and speculator, contributed the initial $500 needed to lease Barnett's land for five years (town history, summarized in Wikipedia from the scholarly literature; BlackPast names the same three men as the founding group). Thomas M. Haynes, a Black farmer and entrepreneur from Texas, was chosen as townsite manager (National Register nomination; Oklahoma Historical Society marker).

The chronology of the founding season matters because the sources date three different events. The townsite was established in August 1903 (Oklahoma Historical Society marker). After a post office was approved in July 1903, the town officially opened for settlement on September 26, 1903, beginning as a camp of Black railroad construction hands (National Register nomination; town history). Haynes advertised the town across the South and Southwest as a place where Black people could govern themselves, and settlers came from Georgia, Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and Florida (National Register nomination). On September 22, 1904, a formal opening of the town was held (National Register nomination). The founding families named in the nomination, the Barnetts, Walkers, Graysons, and Johnsons, became the town's leading citizens (National Register nomination). Additions brought the townsite to about 300 acres (National Register nomination).

One of the most influential early settlers was David J. Turner, a businessman who served repeatedly as mayor and councilman and worked as a pharmacist and, later, bank president (town history). Hilliard Taylor built a thriving cotton ginning business (town history). Boley was incorporated on May 11, 1905 (Oklahoma Historical Society marker).
